# Working notes

## Milestone 1 — Community and candidate distinctions

**Community:** IMDb user reviews (public, no login needed), from 10 films across
several genres. Reviews are copied by hand: 20 per film, sampled evenly across star ratings(10/8/6/4/2 or 10/7/4/1), skipping reviews flagged as spoilers.

**Films:**

1. Obsession — Horror
2. Resident Evil — Horror
3. Fall 2: Deadpoint — Thriller
4. Project Hail Mary — Sci-Fi
5. Spider-Man — Sci-Fi
6. The Wandering Earth — Sci-Fi
7. The Shawshank Redemption — Drama
8. Life is Beautiful — Drama
9. Your Name — Animation
10. WALL·E — Animation

**Candidate distinctions I saw while reading:**

1. Many reviews use heavy punctuation (like "!!!") and ALL-CAPS words. Both signal
   strong emotion, and it can go either way: intense praise or intense,
   exasperated criticism.
2. Low-rated reviews use the word "overrated" more often, and some contain
   swear words.
3. When the writer loves or hates the film, they often say so right in the
   title: "Amazing", "The Best", "10 out of 10", "Overrated", "Not worth it".
4. Many one-sentence or very short reviews only say the film is good or bad.
   They give no reasons and mention nothing specific from the film.
5. When a review names another film as a comparison or reference, a few
   mention it in passing, but most use it to argue that this film is better or
   worse, or to explain why the writer liked or disliked it.
6. When a review names the director or an actor, it is usually not a passing
   mention. The name comes with a description or a line of reasoning, and
   serves as evidence.
7. Reviews that point to specific parts of the film itself (plot, acting,
   score, ending) mostly use them as evidence to judge the film. Reviews that
   describe the audience's experience and feelings (laughing, crying) mostly
   read as experience reports or emotional reports.
8. Besides judging the film, some reviews comment on the rating itself and say
   it can't be trusted: "Really? 10/10?", "Do not believe the rating!!! Not
   worth it!", "Dont Look on the Bad Reviews here."

**Grouped into three dimensions:**

- Emotional intensity (1, 2, 3)
- Evidence and specificity (4, 5, 6, 7)
- What the review is responding to: the film, the writer's own experience, or
  the rating and other reviewers (7, 8)
